Split panes

A tab can hold several terminals at once, side by side or one above the other – a server's log next to its shell, or three servers in view while you broadcast to them.

Splitting

To… Press Or right-click into the terminal
Put a new pane right of this one Ctrl+Shift+D Split right
Put a new pane below this one Ctrl+Shift+Alt+D Split down

The pane you split halves its room with the new one. What the new pane runs follows the one you split:

  • A local shell: the same shell, in the same working directory (when the shell reports it, see Shell integration).
  • An SSH session: a new connection to the same host with the same login. Prompts for passphrases or passwords come up again, in the new pane.

A pane that would end up smaller than 8 columns or 2 rows isn't split.

Moving around

  • Click into a pane to give it the keyboard.
  • Ctrl+Alt+← → ↑ ↓ moves to the neighbouring pane in that direction.

The pane with the keyboard shows a solid cursor, the others an outlined one. The tab bar and the window title show its title. Everything that acts on "the terminal" acts on that pane: copy and paste, search, prompt jumps, the command buttons, the port forwards listed in the SSH section. Only the mouse wheel scrolls whichever pane is under the mouse.

Resizing

Drag the thin line between two panes. The mouse pointer turns into a resize arrow over it. Each side keeps room for at least a few columns or rows.

With the keyboard, Ctrl+Shift+Alt+arrow moves a line of the focused pane that way, two columns or one row at a time. At the edge of the window the line on the other side moves instead, so the key always moves a line in its own direction.

Swapping

Swap with the pane on the left / right / above / below lets the focused pane trade places with its neighbour; the keyboard goes along. It has no default shortcut – give it one under Settings → Shortcuts → Split panes, or run it from the command palette.

Maximizing

Ctrl+Shift+Enter shows the focused pane over the whole tab; press it again for all panes. Moving to another pane with Ctrl+Alt+arrow keeps the tab maximized and shows that one instead. Splitting, or closing the focused pane, brings all panes back.

Closing

  • Ctrl+Shift+W or Close pane in the right-click menu closes the focused pane; its neighbour takes over the room.
  • Ending the shell (exit, Ctrl+D) closes its pane too — unless it failed within five seconds of starting: then the pane stays with a line naming the status, so the error can be read.
  • A tab closes with its last pane.
  • Ctrl+Shift+Alt+W, the tab's × or a middle click on it close the whole tab with all its panes.

Broadcast

The broadcast group is made of terminals, not tabs: Ctrl+Shift+I puts the focused pane in or takes it out. Panes in the group get a red frame, and their tab a red line in the tab bar. Typing into a pane of the group reaches every terminal in it, in this tab and in others. See One command on many servers.

Shortcuts

All of these can be changed under Settings → Shortcuts → Split panes or in config.toml: split_right, split_down, close_pane, focus_pane_left, focus_pane_right, focus_pane_up, focus_pane_down, resize_pane_left, resize_pane_right, resize_pane_up, resize_pane_down, swap_pane_left, swap_pane_right, swap_pane_up, swap_pane_down, zoom_pane. See Keyboard shortcuts.

Limits

  • The layout is saved with the session and comes back at the next start, but every pane starts a fresh shell or connection. See Restoring the last session.
