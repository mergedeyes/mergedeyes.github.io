Alerting recipes

Ready-made notification setups for situations most teams run into. Each recipe has the goal, the configuration, what happens over time, and what to know about acknowledgements, problems that fix themselves, and restarts.

The fields used here are explained in notify. The contacts in the examples (team, lead, ...) need to exist as contacts or contact groups.

Three things are true for every recipe:

  • Only confirmed problems notify. A problem counts once it is HARD, after max_attempts failed checks in a row. The times below start there, not at the first failed check.
  • Acknowledging stops everything for that problem: reminders and escalations. The RECOVERY is still sent, and it ends the acknowledgement.
  • A recovery goes to whoever got the problem. Rules with after only send the RECOVERY to contacts who were told about the problem.

Quiet start, team reminders, then the team lead

Small problems often fix themselves. Give them 15 minutes. If the problem is still there, tell the team, and remind them every 30 minutes. If nobody has acknowledged it after 1.5 hours, tell the team lead.

[[notify]]
name = "team"
contact_groups = ["team"]
after = "15m"
repeat = "30m"

[[notify]]
name = "teamlead"
contacts = ["lead"]
after = "1h30m"

For a problem that is confirmed at 10:00:

Time Who gets it
10:00 nobody yet
10:15 team
10:45 team ("is still CRITICAL")
11:15 team
11:30 lead
11:45 team, then every 30 minutes
  • Acknowledged at 10:50: nothing more is sent. The lead never hears about it. At recovery, the team gets the RECOVERY and the lead doesn't.
  • Acknowledged at 12:00, OK at 12:20: team and lead both get the RECOVERY, since both got the problem.
  • OK again at 10:10: nobody gets anything, neither the problem nor the recovery.
  • WARNING turns into CRITICAL: that is a new problem. The team gets it 15 minutes later, the lead 1.5 hours later.
  • Add repeat = "1h" to the teamlead rule if the lead should be reminded too.

Remind until someone acknowledges

Tell the on-call person at once, and again every 30 minutes until they acknowledge it.

[[notify]]
contacts = ["oncall"]
repeat = "30m"

The reminder says how long the problem has been open: PROBLEM: web01/http is still CRITICAL (for 1h 30m). Acknowledge the problem to stop the reminders.

Escalation ladder

On-call first. The team lead if nobody reacts within 30 minutes. The manager only for critical problems that are still open after 2 hours.

[[notify]]
contacts = ["oncall"]
repeat = "30m"

[[notify]]
contacts = ["lead"]
after = "30m"
repeat = "1h"

[[notify]]
contacts = ["manager"]
states = ["critical", "down"]
after = "2h"

Each level is one rule with its own after. states keeps WARNING problems away from the manager; include down so hosts that are DOWN count as well.

On-call at night, the team during business hours

During business hours, the team gets problems. At night and on weekends, only the on-call person. Whatever is still broken in the morning goes to the team when they start.

[timeperiod.workhours]
title = "Business hours"
schedule = [{ days = ["mon", "tue", "wed", "thu", "fri"], from = "08:00", to = "18:00" }]

[timeperiod.offhours]
title = "Nights and weekends"
schedule = [
  { days = ["mon", "tue", "wed", "thu", "fri"], from = "00:00", to = "08:00" },
  { days = ["mon", "tue", "wed", "thu", "fri"], from = "18:00", to = "00:00" },
  { days = ["sat", "sun"], from = "00:00", to = "00:00" },
]

[[notify]]
contact_groups = ["team"]
period = "workhours"

[[notify]]
contacts = ["oncall"]
period = "offhours"
repeat = "30m"

For a problem that starts on Tuesday at 03:00:

Time Who gets it
03:00 oncall
03:30, 04:00, ... oncall again, every 30 minutes
08:00 team; oncall's reminders pause until 18:00
  • Acknowledged by on-call at 04:10: nothing more is sent. The team doesn't get it at 08:00 either; someone is already on it.
  • Fixed at 06:00: on-call gets the RECOVERY. The team never hears of it.
  • Problem at 17:55, fixed at 19:00: the team got the problem, so they get the RECOVERY too, even after hours. On-call gets the problem at 18:00, when their time period begins, and the RECOVERY at 19:00.
  • The time zone is timezone in watcher.toml, or the system's.

Managers: critical problems only, no recoveries

Managers want to know about real outages, not about every WARNING, and they don't need the all-clear.

[[notify]]
contacts = ["manager"]
states = ["critical", "down"]
on = ["problem"]

Without on, the rule would also send recoveries, flapping and downtime messages.

Chat for everything, email only for critical problems

The team chat gets every notification. Email only for CRITICAL and DOWN, so the inbox stays quiet.

A contact gets the same message on all its channels, so use two contacts:

[contact.ops-chat]
slack = "https://hooks.slack.com/services/T0000/B0000/XXXXXXXX"

[contact.ops-mail]
email = "ops@example.com"

[[notify]]
contacts = ["ops-chat"]

[[notify]]
contacts = ["ops-mail"]
states = ["critical", "down"]
on = ["problem", "recovery"]

teams, telegram or any other channel works the same way. For paging, a contact with pagerduty in place of email opens an incident for each critical problem and resolves it on recovery; a contact with jira opens a ticket instead (see Jira).

Each team gets its own services

The DBAs get database problems, the web team gets web problems, and the NOC sees everything.

[[notify]]
contact_groups = ["dba"]
services = { checks = ["pgsql", "mysql"] }

[[notify]]
contact_groups = ["web"]
services = { groups = ["websites"] }

[[notify]]
contacts = ["noc"]

A rule with services never gets host notifications (host DOWN and UP). If the DBAs should also hear when a database server goes down, add a rule with hosts = { groups = ["db-servers"] } and without services.

Customers see only their own hosts

Each customer gets notifications about their own hosts only.

[[notify]]
contacts = ["acme-it"]
hosts = { groups = ["acme"] }
states = ["critical", "down"]

[[notify]]
contacts = ["globex-it"]
hosts = { name = "globex-*" }

See Customers (MSP) for a complete setup.

Maintenance windows without noise

No alerts during the Sunday night patch window, but problems that are still there afterwards must not get lost.

[downtimegroup.patching]
hostgroups = ["linux"]
schedule = [{ days = ["sun"], from = "02:00", to = "04:00" }]

[[notify]]
contacts = ["oncall"]
repeat = "1h"

During the window, problems are held back. The catch: a problem that started in the window had its PROBLEM held back, and nothing new happens when the window ends. Without repeat or after, nobody would be told. With repeat = "1h", it is sent once it has been open for an hour. So a server that broke at 02:30 is reported at 04:00, right after the window.

The rule also sends DOWNTIMESTART and DOWNTIMEEND messages. The end message names the state at that moment. Use on = ["problem", "recovery"] if you don't want them.

One alert for the router, not fifty

When the branch office router dies, every host behind it goes DOWN. On-call should get one notification about the router, not one per host.

[[dependency]]
name = "behind-gw"
on = "gw"
hosts = { groups = ["branch-office"] }

[[notify]]
contacts = ["oncall"]
repeat = "1h"

While gw is DOWN (confirmed), the hosts in branch-office and their services send nothing; oncall gets gw is DOWN and works on that. Checks keep running, so the web UI still shows what is really broken.

The catch is the same as with maintenance windows: a host behind the router that is still broken after the router recovered had its PROBLEM held back, and nothing new happens when the router comes back. With repeat = "1h", it is sent once it has been open for an hour. Details: dependency.

Flapping services

A service that keeps changing between OK and CRITICAL should not send a message every few minutes.

This needs no configuration. When a service starts flapping, Watcher sends one FLAPPINGSTART and holds back its problems and recoveries. When it calms down, it sends FLAPPINGSTOP. Reminders and escalations wait too. Use on = ["problem", "recovery"] on a rule if its contacts don't want the flapping messages.

Checking your setup

Who gets notified about a service, and why:

watcher config explain web01/http
└─ notifications
   ├─ notify team: matched: all; escalates after 15m; repeats every 30m  notify.toml:1
   │  └─ contactgroup team                                  contacts.toml:5
   └─ notify teamlead: matched: all; escalates after 1h 30m  notify.toml:7
      └─ contact lead                                       contacts.toml:1

Whether a contact's channels work:

watcher notify test lead

What was actually sent: the Notifications panel on each host and service page, or the notification log.

Restarts

With a database, restarting Watcher changes nothing in these recipes. Watcher reads from the notification log who already got which problem:

  • Nothing is sent twice.
  • A reminder or escalation that fell due while Watcher was stopped goes out right after the start, once.
  • Contacts who got a problem before the restart still get its RECOVERY.

Without a database, open problems start their time again after a restart, and escalations sent before it get no RECOVERY.
